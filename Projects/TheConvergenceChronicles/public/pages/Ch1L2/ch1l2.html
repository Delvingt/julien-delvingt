Chapter 1: The Valve

Controlling Flow with Valves

Now that you've taken your first steps as an engineer at the Convergence Institute, it's time to learn about valves — devices that restrict and control the flow of water through a system.

A valve acts as a devider. When a water pipe is obstructed by a valve, the water flow diminishes according to the valve's opening setting (k). A gain of 1.0 means fully open — all water passes through. A gain of 0.0 means fully closed — no water passes.

This time, the blocks are not connected. You'll need to drag a Valve from the Toolbox onto the canvas and wire the blocks together by dragging from one block's output port to another block's input port.

Objective

Drag the Valve from the Toolbox onto the canvas. Wire Pump → Valve → Flow Sensor. Set the valve gain to achieve exactly 0.5 L/s of water flow.

The Toolbox

1 / 3

On the left side of the Flow Editor you'll see the Toolbox. It contains blocks you can use in your design.

  • Drag & Drop: Click and drag a block from the Toolbox onto the canvas to place it
  • You can place multiple copies of the same block if needed

For this level, the Toolbox contains a Valve (Gain) block. Drag it onto the canvas!

Wiring Blocks Together

2 / 3

Blocks have ports — small circles on their edges:

  • Output ports (right side) send signals out
  • Input ports (left side) receive signals in

To connect two blocks, click and drag from an output port to an input port. A wire will appear linking them.

Wire the blocks in order: Pump → Valve → Flow Sensor

Understanding the Valve

3 / 3

The Valve block is a gain block — it multiplies the input signal by a value k.

  • k = 1.0: Fully open — all flow passes through
  • k = 0.5: Half open — flow is halved
  • k = 0.0: Fully closed — no flow

Hint: The pump outputs 1.0 L/s. What gain value gives you 0.5 L/s?
